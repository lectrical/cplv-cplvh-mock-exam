Module 1: Dangers of Electricity

Electricity kills and injures through four primary hazards: Electric Shock, Arc Flash / Burns, Fire & Explosion, and Secondary Injuries (e.g., falling from height after an involuntary muscle jerk).

50 Hz AC CurrentPhysiological Effect
1 – 5 mAThreshold of perception; mild tingle.
10 – 15 mALet-go threshold — muscle tetany prevents releasing the conductor.
30 mARespiratory paralysis; standard RCD trip rating for additional protection.
50 mA+Ventricular fibrillation — fatal heart rhythm disruption.
  • 3 Severity Factors: Current magnitude, duration of contact, and path through the body.
  • Direct vs. Indirect Contact: Touching a bare live part vs. touching a normally dead metallic casing made live by a fault.
  • Arc Flash: Generates radiant heat up to 20,000 °C, blast pressure, and molten metal.
Emergency Action: NEVER touch a casualty still in contact with a live source. Isolate/switch off the supply first, then summon medical aid and begin CPR.

Module 5: Safe Isolation & LOTO (Prove–Test–Prove)

  1. Identify & Liaise: Identify the circuit and gain permission. Healthcare: Mandatory agreement from Clinical/Ward Manager first.
  2. Isolate All Sources: Switch off main supply + all back-feeds (generators, UPS, IPS, dual feeders) and verify capacitor discharge. Never use emergency stops or single-pole wall switches for isolation.
  3. Secure (LOTO): Apply a unique-key personal padlock (or multi-lock hasp for multiple teams) and post a Caution Notice. Withdrawn fuses require a lockable insert and retaining the fuse.
  4. Prove – Test – Prove: Check two-pole GS38 indicator on a proving unit → test all conductors (3 tests on 1-phase; 10 tests on 3-phase+N) → re-check on proving unit.
  5. Portable Earths (if required): Connect to earth first; disconnect from earth last.
Instant Fail: If your voltage indicator fails the second proving-unit check, treat the circuit as LIVE. Never cut off a lost-key padlock — follow the AP's formal lock-removal procedure.

Module 6: Live Working, GS38 & Healthcare Systems

EAWR Reg 14 — All 3 Conditions Required for Live Working: (1) Unreasonable in all circumstances to be dead; (2) Reasonable in all circumstances to work live; (3) Suitable precautions (PPE, barriers, ASP) are taken. Convenience never justifies live work.
  • GS38 Test Probes: Max 4 mm exposed metal tip (2 mm recommended), insulated finger barriers, HBC fuses up to 500 mA. CAT IV required at main LV intake/origin.
  • Banned for Proving Dead: Digital multimeters, single-pole neon screwdrivers, and non-contact volt sticks (screened by SWA armouring/conduit).
  • Live PPE Standards: Insulated tools (BS EN 60900), Class 0 gloves up to 1000 V AC (BS EN 60903 — visual & air-inflation roll test before use), insulating matting (BS EN 61111). Remove all metallic jewellery; assign an Accompanying Safety Person (ASP).
Healthcare LV Systems (CPLVH / HTM 06-01): Operating Theatres & ICUs are Group 2 Medical Locations. They use a Medical Isolated Power Supply (IPS) with an Insulation Monitoring Device (IMD) so a first earth fault alarms without tripping life-support power. Always verify Essential (generator/UPS backed) vs. Non-Essential supplies.

Module 7: Testing Prior to Re-energisation & Restoration

  • Dead Pre-Energisation Tests (BS 7671): Continuity, Insulation Resistance (minimum 1.0 MΩ at 500 V DC for 230/400 V circuits), and Polarity (single-pole switches/fuses in line conductor only).
  • Restoration Sequence (after PTW Cancellation): (1) Remove temporary earths (earth last) → (2) Remove locks & notices → (3) Re-energise.
Fault on Re-energisation: If a breaker trips instantaneously on re-energisation, NEVER reset it blindly. Isolate, lock off, prove dead, and investigate the fault.
